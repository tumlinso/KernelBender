# Independent review of design probes v1

## Scope and limits

The two paired blocks used the same three prompts within each A/B pair and the same wrapper hash; prompt hashes match across arms. A had the competent CUDA/KDA, KernelWiki, ncu-report-skill, and Atlas sources, including machine-aligned CUDA design. B added KernelBender commit `25b4b16`. Both used the configured Luna implementer at xhigh and the same 300-second / 12-command ceiling. This is a behavioral comparison of design briefs only: no kernel, compiler, sanitizer, profiler, or GPU result was produced. The source records and limits are in `trial-policy.json`, `input-manifests.json`, and the four receipts.

## What the paired briefs show

| Probe | Behavior in A and B | Comparative evidence |
|---|---|---|
| E01, consumer output | Both retain the direct FP32 chain as a baseline, offer epoch-prepared `A*B` only when the intermediate is unobservable and tolerance permits reassociation, charge preparation/storage, and fall back when parameters update each batch or `X*A` must be rounded. See [A, lines 5–9](probe-1-A/briefs.md#L5-L9) and [B, lines 3–9](probe-1-B/briefs.md#L3-L9). | B spells out the cost comparison and temporary traffic more directly. The structural proposal and semantic gates are already present in A; no design family is unique to B. |
| E02, per-position state | Both preserve every final byte and the count, use a two-plane packed construction with halo recomputation, explicitly handle periodic wrap and partial tails, and charge conversion/reduction costs. Both adapt when intermediate states become observable or masks change. See [A, lines 13–17](probe-1-A/briefs.md#L13-L17) and [B, lines 13–19](probe-1-B/briefs.md#L13-L19). | B adds a concrete shared-memory byte-tile route and details the bit-plane output/count construction and tail sizes. A already supplies the packed representation, halo, wrap, validation, and fallback conditions. |
| E03, packed-weight reuse | Both compare direct signed-nibble decoding with one shared FP32 prepared matrix, account for query reuse and update invalidation, and preserve FP32 `float(q)*scale` before accumulation. See [A, lines 21–25](probe-1-A/briefs.md#L21-L25) and [B, lines 21–29](probe-1-B/briefs.md#L21-L29). | B makes the 32 KiB footprint, two consumers, and Q=1 versus Q=64 comparison explicit, and cautions against transferring an FP16 result. | E05, evidence to experiment | Both treat the stated timing and replay counters as fixture observations, keep causality unresolved, require a safe current baseline, and distinguish useful parallelism from conversion/issue cost with controlled evidence. Across all six prompts, B shows bounded, architecture-aware behavior and improves specificity on lifecycle accounting, access boundaries, and causal experiments. A independently reaches the same central design families and most of the same semantic safeguards. The briefs therefore demonstrate useful design behavior in B, but do not establish that KernelBender adds a materially new feasible design over this already competent A arm. They also do not answer whether a B design becomes a better valid implementation under equal search budgets.

## Cost, caveats, and correction need

Observed wall time was higher for B in both blocks: 178.5 s versus 147.0 s in block 1 (+21.4%), and 130.0 s versus 105.3 s in block 2 (+23.4%). Token costs are unavailable. Command runtime was a small fraction of elapsed time. Brief byte counts are recorded in `observed-costs.json`, but length is not a quality measure and is not used as one here. Block 1 A has one logged failed `help` command; the run note records a B1 zsh unmatched-glob before wrapper invocation that read no contents. That pre-wrapper event is outside the wrapper command log. The namespace wrapper was exercised; broader model-tool confinement remains instruction-controlled, not adversarially established.

I found no consequential mathematical, numerical, architecture, or control failure that requires changing the frozen skill before the native pilot. The probes are designs and proposed tests, not verification: E01 dimensions/target, E03 measured crossover, E04 exact operator and participant contract, and E05 actual source attribution remain workload-dependent and correctly unresolved. Proceeding to the already bounded native evaluation can test whether B’s added detail changes implementation outcomes; these probes alone support no skill-level performance or added-value claim.
